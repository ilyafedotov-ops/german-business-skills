## 1 · English launch post

Running a small business in Germany comes with a second job: paperwork. Often in a second language.

So over the past month I built German Business Kit: 7 open-source AI skills that turn Claude or Codex into a step-by-step assistant for sole traders in Germany (Freiberufler and Gewerbe).

Every answer comes with its receipt:
→ the official pages it actually checked (§18 EStG, §19 UStG, ELSTER, your city's service portal)
→ what it couldn't check, said out loud
→ and who sends in the end: you.

What it helps with:
→ Starting out: Freiberufler or Gewerbe? Registration and Fragebogen worksheets
→ Daily work: proposals, invoices, payment follow-ups, letters
→ Accounting: receipts, bank matching, month close, in the system you choose
→ Tax: sourced research, checked estimates, return prep, replies to the Finanzamt
→ Services and changes: banks, insurance, software, a first hire, a pause or closing

It saves documents, decisions and next steps in one private folder, so next month it picks up where you stopped.

Swipe for three real questions from Berlin, Hamburg and Munich: fictional people, real answers, shortened.

Honest status: early preview (0.14). Ask in English, German or Russian; guides and report templates come in all three. AI assistance, not tax advice. No ELSTER transmission, no complete tax engine. What the assistant reads goes to its AI provider, so start with a made-up example.

## 4 · "Real questions" post (three images)

I asked my German Business Kit three questions people in Germany really ask. Fictional people, real answers, shortened:

🧾 Berlin: "I translate on the side. Freiberufler or Gewerbe?"
→ Freiberufler. It quoted §18 EStG, which names translators, explained the one registration via ELSTER and its one-month deadline, and asked follow-up questions that would change the details.

🧾 Hamburg: "€22,000 in my first year. Do I have to charge VAT?"
→ Most likely not: the first-year limit is €25,000 of money actually received. When the Finance Ministry's guidance letter wouldn't load, it said so instead of pretending it had read it.

🧾 Munich: "My profit dropped. Can I pay less in advance?"
→ Yes, under §37(3) EStG. It flagged that the next payment was due in 15 days and drafted the German letter, stamped "Entwurf, nicht versandt". (Asked in German, it refused to estimate the new tax until it knew filing status and other income.)

Sources you can check, assumptions stated, limits admitted, and you decide what gets sent.

AI assistance, not tax advice.
