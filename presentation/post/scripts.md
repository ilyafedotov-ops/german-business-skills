## YouTube walkthrough (16:9, about 4–5 minutes)

**Y1 · Intro (0:00)**
If you work for yourself in Germany, as a Freiberufler or with a small Gewerbe, you know the paperwork. Before your first invoice, there's a tax registration questionnaire. Then come invoices with mandatory details, receipts, bank statements, tax returns, and letters from the Finanzamt, often in a language that isn't your first. I wanted an assistant that helps with all of it, one clear step at a time, without pretending to be a tax adviser. So I built German Business Kit.

**Y2 · What it is**
German Business Kit is a set of seven skills for Claude and Codex. A skill is a package of instructions, references and templates that the assistant loads when your request needs it. So there's no new app to learn. You work in Claude Code, the Claude app, or Codex, and you ask in plain English, German or Russian. Guides and reports come in all three.

**Y3 · How it works**
Every task follows the same rhythm. One: you ask in your own words. For example: help me turn these agreed terms into a German proposal. Two: the assistant clarifies. It reuses facts you've already confirmed, asks only for what's missing, then prepares a readable draft and names the next step. Three: for ongoing work, it saves results, confirmed facts and open questions in your private business folder. Next month, it continues from there instead of starting over.

**Y4 · Seven skills**
The skills follow the life of a sole trader. Start helps you describe your activity, work out whether it's Freiberufler or Gewerbe, and prepare registration worksheets. Operations handles proposals, invoices, payment follow-ups and letters. Accounting organizes receipts and bank exports, matches them, and prepares the month in the system you choose, including software you already use. Tax researches the rules for your period, makes estimates with recorded checks, and prepares returns and Finanzamt replies. Services compares banks, insurance and software. Changes plans a second activity, a first hire, a pause, or closing. And the main kit skill is the front door that routes mixed requests.

**Y5 · Your business folder**
Your business folder keeps everything together in five everyday areas: inbox, documents, accounts, taxes and business. Originals are preserved and numbered. One profile covers all your activities and years, and an overview page shows where you are. The kit can also make a full, verified backup, and restore it into a new location.

**Y6a · Real apps: skills**
Here's what that looks like in the real apps. In Codex, the kit shows up as seven skills in your project, each with a plain description of what it's for.

**Y6b · Real apps: ask**
In Claude, you ask in your own words. In this fictional example, a creator makes videos, and the first brands want to pay for posts.

**Y6c · Real apps: next step**
Instead of a lecture, you get one small first step, and only the three questions needed to take it.

**Y6d · Real apps: German**
And it works in German, too: the same skills, the same private folder.

**Yq1 · Real question: Berlin**
Now some real questions, each asked in a fresh session with the released kit. A translator in Berlin asks: Freiberufler or Gewerbe? The answer quotes the law that names translators, explains the one registration needed through ELSTER and its one-month deadline, and links to the official pages it opened.

**Yq2 · Real question: Hamburg**
A web designer in Hamburg asks about the small business VAT exemption. The answer explains the first-year limit, what happens when a payment crosses it, and when regular VAT starts. And when the Finance Ministry's guidance wouldn't load, it said so, instead of guessing.

**Yq3 · Real question: Munich**
A photographer in Munich, whose profit dropped, asks about quarterly tax prepayments. The answer estimates the likely tax with this year's tariff, checks the arithmetic two ways, flags that the next payment is due in fifteen days, and drafts the German letter, marked as a draft, not sent.

**Y7 · Principle**
One principle runs through the whole kit: it prepares, you decide and send. Extraction, proposal, review, booking, authorization, submission and the authority's answer are separate steps, each with its own evidence. A polished draft is never treated as a filed return. You sign in to ELSTER, your bank or your accounting software yourself, you never type passwords or TANs into the chat, and you make the final click.

**Y8 · Install**
Installing takes a few minutes. In the Claude app, open Customize, then Plugins, add the marketplace from GitHub, and install German Business Kit. In Claude Code, it's two commands. In Codex, the released skills are copied into your project. Or paste the install request from the README, and let your assistant do the setup. Then start a fresh chat, and say: start the welcome setup.

**Y9 · Limits and call to action**
A few honest limits. This is an early preview. It's AI assistance, not professional tax advice, and AI can get calculations and deadlines wrong. It doesn't send anything to ELSTER, and it isn't a complete tax engine. Whatever the assistant reads is processed by its AI provider, so start with a made-up example. The kit is open source under the MIT licence, and the link is in the description. If you run a small business in Germany, try it, and tell me what's missing. Danke, and bis bald.
